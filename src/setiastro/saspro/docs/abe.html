Automatic (Dynamic) Background Extraction — ADBE

Models the sky background — gradients, vignetting, light pollution — with a polynomial plus an optional radial-basis refinement, and removes it while preserving star colour and linear photometry. An optional multiscale stage additionally removes sharp multiplicative defects (dust motes, reflections, filter-edge shadows) that smooth fits can't model. Non-destructive: nothing changes until Apply.

1. Using the tool

Set the model parameters, optionally guide the sampling (exclude regions or place points by hand), Preview, then Apply. The preview is fully interactive; the sampling behaviour depends on whether you're in Auto or Manual mode.

For RGB data, combine R/G/B into one colour image before running ADBE. Running it on separate mono channels can leave colour artefacts at saturated stars once they're recombined.

Sample points: Auto vs Manual

This is the key choice, and it changes what your mouse does in the preview:

  • Auto mode — ADBE places all the sample points for you. You don't place points yourself; instead you steer the sampler by drawing exclusion areas on the preview (see below) so it stays off bright signal. The # sample points value controls how many it places.
  • Manual mode — you place every sample point by hand on the preview. Exclusion areas aren't used here (there's no auto-sampler to keep out), and # sample points is ignored.
  • Switching modes clears the other's guidance — going Auto→Manual drops any exclusion areas, and Manual→Auto drops your hand-placed points (Auto will generate its own). So decide the mode first, then do the guidance work.

Auto mode — drawing exclusion areas

Exclusion areas are regions where no sample points will be placed — draw them around bright nebulosity, a large galaxy, or anything that isn't sky background, so the gradient fit isn't contaminated.

  • Left-click-drag on the preview to outline a region; it shows red/dashed while you draw.
  • Release the left button (or right-click) to close the polygon — it fills green and becomes active.
  • Draw as many as you like; Clear Exclusions removes them all.
  • The sampler already avoids the brightest ~50% of each image quartile on its own — exclusion areas are for the larger, obvious regions that threshold won't catch.
Exclusion areas do double duty when Multiscale Gradient Refinement is on: the same polygons that keep the sampler off your galaxy also protect that galaxy from the multiscale correction (see below), so you can draw once and both stages respect it.

Manual mode — placing points by hand

In Manual mode you drop sample points exactly where you want the background measured:

  • Left-click on background to add a sample point (drawn as a yellow square).
  • Right-click to remove the nearest point.

Three helpers seed a starting set and switch you to Manual so you can refine by hand:

  • Place Grid (N×N) — drops a regular grid of points across the image (skipping any that fall inside exclusion areas you'd drawn in Auto), then switches to Manual.
  • Show Auto Points — runs the automatic sampler and converts its chosen points into editable manual points, then switches to Manual — so you can start from ADBE's picks and nudge or delete individual ones.
  • Clear Sample Points — removes all manual points.

Multiscale Gradient Refinement (optional)

The polynomial and RBF stages model smooth background. They cannot represent sharp-edged, multiplicative defects in the imaging train — dust motes (soft dark donuts), internal reflections / ghosts, and filter-edge shadows (coarse frame darkening). These are throughput losses: light that should have landed is attenuated by some factor less than one. A smooth surface fit just slides underneath them; multiscale refinement is built to catch them.

When enabled, it runs after the normal background removal, on the already-flattened result:

Dust motes, reflections and filter edges live in the mid-to-coarse layers; 6–8 is a good starting band on a full-size frame. Protect k (MAD)Signal-protection threshold in MAD units. Pixels brighter than median + k·MAD are treated as signal, masked out and interpolated across so they aren't modelled as gradient. k = 1–2 protects aggressively (safest for faint signal), k = 3 is balanced, k = 4–5 protects only the brightest cores and lets more extended structure into the estimate.
Protecting a galaxy or nebula. The MAD threshold alone can't both flatten the background and fully protect a faint extended halo — a high k flattens well but can eat the halo. So the drawn exclusion area is honoured at apply time: wherever you've excluded, the original pixels are kept unchanged, no matter what the gradient did there. Draw a polygon around M81/M82 (or your nebula), push k up for a dead-flat field everywhere else, and the object comes back pristine.
Workflow: run ADBE once in Subtract mode with multiscale off and look at the result. If sharp mottling, donuts or a darkened frame edge remain, tick Enable multiscale refinement, draw exclusion areas around any bright objects, set the band to 6–8, and Preview. Adjust the band and k until the background is flat and the objects are untouched.

Navigating the preview

  • Zoom — mouse wheel (anchored at the cursor; hold Ctrl for finer steps), or the Zoom In / Zoom Out / Fit buttons. Wheel-zoom works even with the cursor over the scrollbars.
  • Pan — middle-drag, or Ctrl + left-drag (works in either mode without adding points or exclusions).
  • AutoStretch (toolbar toggle) — screen-stretches the preview so faint gradients are visible; it never affects the data that gets processed.

Running it

Preview computes the correction without touching the document; Apply commits an “ABE” step (and, if asked, the background document); Close dismisses. The grip (bottom-left) drags to the canvas to mint an ADBE shortcut with these settings, or drops on an image to apply headlessly.

2. Technical details

A two-stage background model is fit on a downsampled copy, in a stretched domain for sampling accuracy, then un-stretched and removed in linear space so photometry and star colour survive. An optional multiscale stage then removes sharp multiplicative defects in log space.

2.1 Two-stage background model

The smooth component is a 2-D polynomial of the chosen degree d, least-squares-fit to the sampled background values z:

B(x,y)=\sum_{p+q\leq d} c_{pq}\,x^{p}\,y^{q}\,,\qquad \min_{c}\;\|A\,c-z\|^{2}

If RBF refinement is on, a second surface is fit to what the polynomial left behind, using a multiquadric radial basis over the sample points:

\phi(r)=\sqrt{r^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}\,,\qquad B_{\mathrm{rbf}}(x)=\sum_i w_i\,\phi\!\left(\|x-x_i\|\right)

The RBF stage is capped at ~1 megapixel (downsampling further if needed) to bound memory, and its smoothing scales with the sample count so the control feels consistent regardless of how many points there are. The total model is B = B_poly + B_rbf.

2.2 Smart sample placement

In Auto mode the sampler seeds points at the corners, along the borders, and within each image quartile, then refines each one by gradient descent to the dimmest nearby spot — it repeatedly steps to whichever neighbour has the lowest local patch-median luminance, so samples land on background rather than on stars or nebulosity:

Y=0.2989\,R+0.5870\,G+0.1140\,B

Within each quartile the brightest ~50% of pixels are excluded before random selection (bright-region avoidance), and any user exclusion polygons remove their pixels from the candidate pool. At every chosen point the background value is the median of the surrounding patch, which rejects stars and hot pixels.

2.3 Fit stretched, remove linear

Gradients are easiest to sample on stretched data, but must be removed from linear data to keep photometry intact. ADBE does both: it fits the model on an auto-stretched copy, then un-stretches the background model back to linear and removes it from the original linear image. The fitting stretch is the same analytic midtones transfer used by Statistical Stretch (target median 0.25):

S(x)=\frac{(M-1)\,T\,x}{M\,(T+x-1)-T\,x}\qquad(T=0.25)

This “fit in stretched space, subtract in linear space” order is what preserves star colour and linear flux relationships — subtracting a stretched background directly would distort both.

2.4 Correction & rescale

With the linear background model B, the two correction modes are:

\mathrm{subtract:}\quad \mathrm{out}=I-B+\left(\mathrm{med}(I)-\mathrm{med}(I-B)\right)
\mathrm{divide:}\quad \mathrm{out}=\frac{I}{B/\mathrm{med}(B)}\ \ (\text{re-centred on }\mathrm{med}(I))

Both re-centre the result on the original median so overall brightness is preserved. A final clip-free, median-anchored rescale lifts any negatives and compresses the ceiling only if the data exceeds 1.0 — holding the median fixed — so no values are hard-clipped away.

2.5 Multiscale multiplicative refinement

Optical-train defects are multiplicative: the observed image is the true scene times a spatially-varying throughput factor.

I_{\mathrm{obs}} = I_{\mathrm{true}} \cdot G(x,y)

Taking logs turns that product into a sum, so an additive multiscale decomposition can separate the defect by spatial scale consistently with the multiplicative model — a linear decomposition would over- or under-correct depending on local brightness:

\log I_{\mathrm{obs}} = \log I_{\mathrm{true}} + \log G

The stage runs on the already-background-corrected image. First it builds a robust signal-protection mask on the (optionally starless) luminance, so real signal is interpolated across rather than modelled as gradient:

\mathrm{protect} = I > \mathrm{med}(I) + k \cdot \mathrm{MAD}\,,\qquad \mathrm{MAD} = 1.4826 \cdot \mathrm{median}(|I - \mathrm{med}(I)|)

It then decomposes the log-image with an à-trous-style pyramid (successive Gaussian blurs; each detail layer is the difference between consecutive blurs, with the blur scale doubling per layer):

d_k = c_k - \mathcal{B}_{\sigma_k}(c_k),\quad \sigma_k = \sigma_0 \cdot 2^{k}

The selected band of detail layers is summed into the log-gradient, then re-centred to zero mean (unit median in linear space) and exponentiated back:

G_{\log} = \sum_{k=k_{lo}}^{k_{hi}} d_k\,,\qquad G_{\log} \leftarrow G_{\log} - \mathrm{median}(G_{\log})
G = \exp(G_{\log}),\qquad \mathrm{median}(G) \approx 1

Finally the defect is divided out (optionally eased in by a strength blend), and the result is re-centred on the original median:

\mathrm{out} = (1-s)\,I + s\,(I / G)

2.6 Star removal & estimation

Point sources would otherwise dominate the mid-scale layers and be mistaken for structure, so with DarkStar enabled the gradient is estimated on a starless copy — CosmicClarity DarkStar removes the stars, the band is extracted from that, and the resulting gradient map is applied to the original (star-bearing) image. The luminance used for both the protection mask and the decomposition is the Rec. 709 weighting:

Y = 0.2126\,R + 0.7152\,G + 0.0722\,B

The gradient is estimated on luminance and applied to all channels, because these throughput defects are achromatic. The decomposition reuses the same GPU-capable (torch) Gaussian blur as the Multiscale Decomposition tool, and only the layers up to the band's top are computed when the coarse residual isn't needed.

2.7 Protecting excluded regions

The multiscale correction runs across the whole frame, but a hand-drawn exclusion area is honoured as a final composite: wherever the mask marks protection, the original pixels are kept, with a few-pixel feather at the boundary so there's no seam.

\mathrm{out} = (1-m)\cdot\mathrm{corrected} + m\cdot I_{\mathrm{orig}}

Because this is a straight composite from the untouched source, whatever the gradient did inside the excluded region is irrelevant — the galaxy or nebula returns exactly as it was, while the background everywhere else stays fully corrected. The same exclusion polygons drawn for the sampler are reused here, so one drawing protects both stages.

2.8 Masking & notes

  • With an active mask, the correction is blended back against the source over the mask (m·out + (1−m)·src).
  • RGB is fit per channel; the background is upscaled back to full resolution with Lanczos interpolation.
  • Preview and Apply share the same engine; Apply can additionally emit the background as a separate document, and the grip/headless preset stores every parameter (and manual points, when in Manual mode).
  • Multiscale refinement is off by default; when on, its parameters (band, strength, protect-k, DarkStar, residual) are saved with the other ADBE settings and recorded in the applied step's metadata.
