Quick Stack

Pick a set of subs, press one button, get an image. Quick Stack is a fast look at what you have: calibrated with a master dark and flat if you have them, but no weighting and no distortion model, and no calibrated or aligned files on disk. It is not a replacement for the Stacking Suite.

Workflow
Add Files… or Add Folder… (or drop them on the window: lights and master darks and flats together) → check the groups in the list, and the dark and flat under each → optionally choose a Reference → press ⚡ Quick Stack. Each group opens as its own new image in the main window, behind this one, and is listed at the bottom of Quick Stack with a preview: double-click it, or press Show in SASpro, to bring it forward. The stacks are not saved until you save them. Stacking the same frames again opens a second copy, named with _1 on the end.
Looking for the full pipeline? The guided calibrate / register / integrate page is the Pipeline tab of the Stacking Suite (it was called Quick Stack in older versions). This tool is separate.

What it does to each frame

  • Load. FITS, XISF, TIFF and camera RAW, linear, 32-bit.
  • Calibrate, if you loaded masters, on the mosaic and in memory: the dark is subtracted, then the flat is divided out (normalised to its own median, per colour plane for a Bayer sensor). It is the same arithmetic as the Stacking Suite with one difference: nothing is clipped at zero, because a stack has to keep the negative half of the noise.
  • Debayer. One-shot-colour frames are debayered with the bilinear method.
  • Align. A single similarity transform: position, rotation and scale. It comes from the same star matching the registration tools use, including the meridian-flip check. There is no distortion correction, so if your optics' distortion differs between the two pier sides a flip can leave the stars slightly soft, mostly near the corners.
  • Sky offset. One number per colour channel per frame, so that frames with a brighter sky are compared fairly by the trim. Nothing else is normalized.

Masters

Load as many master darks and master flats as you have (FITS, XISF or TIFF, the same size as the subs): one flat per filter, darks for each exposure. Drop them on the window with the lights, or use Add Masters…. Every group in the list shows, right under it, the Dark and the Flat it will be calibrated with, and why:

  • Dark: the same size, then the same gain, then the nearest exposure.
  • Flat: the same size, then the same filter: as written, then written another way (O3 for OIII, H-alpha or Ha 7nm for Ha, S2, Lum, Red…), then named in the file or folder name of a flat that has no FILTER card (MasterFlat_Ha.fits, Flats/OIII/). If you load only one flat, it is used for every group of its size.

The badge beside each says how it was picked: ✓ auto, ≈ matched by another spelling or by the file name, ✎ your choice, or ⚠ when nothing fits or the pick does not quite match (another filter, exposure or gain). To change a group’s pick, open its list and choose another master, None, or Add a master … to load one just for it. Your choice holds for that group until you change it back to Auto; masters of another size are greyed out.

What you add is sorted by its IMAGETYP card, or else by its file or folder name (Flats, Darks 300s, MasterFlat_Ha). Lights go to the list and master darks and flats to the masters. Single dark or flat frames (not masters), bias and flat-dark frames are left out, and the log lists them: Quick Stack calibrates with masters only. A master that says nothing about what it is is asked about when you add it with Add Masters….

The log names the masters each group used and repeats the warnings. A group that nothing fits is stacked without that master. Masters cannot be applied to X-Trans RAW frames.

A master does not have to be stored the way the subs are:

  • A debayered (colour) master on raw one-shot-colour subs gives its Bayer mosaic back: at each pixel, the sample the camera measured there is taken back out of it. That is exact for any demosaic that keeps the measured samples, which SASpro’s all do.
  • A raw mosaic master on subs that were debayered elsewhere is debayered to match, with the pattern on its BAYERPAT card (or the subs’). A dark with no pattern is applied to each colour channel alike; a flat with no pattern is left out, since its colour planes cannot be told apart.

What cannot be made to fit is not guessed at. A master of another size (turned 90°, or at another binning), a dark far brighter than the subs (another camera’s, or in other units), or a file that cannot be read is left out, the log says why, and the group is stacked without it. A master never stops a stack: a single sub that does not fit the masters is skipped and listed, like any other bad sub.

Combining: trimmed mean

For every pixel the brightest and faintest samples are dropped, the same number off each end, and the rest are averaged. That removes hot pixels, cosmic rays and bright streaks that appear in only one or two frames. A trail in several frames is weakened, not removed. The noise is close to a plain average’s: up to about 5% higher on the smallest stacks, next to nothing on deep ones.

Frames at that pixelDropped
Fewer than 6Nothing (plain average)
6 to 14The highest and the lowest
15 or moreThe two highest and the two lowest

Groups

Frames are stacked together only when their filter, exposure, size, binning and Bayer pattern match. The list shows the groups; each one becomes one image. Select rows and press Remove Selected to leave frames out.

Reference

If you do not choose one, the first frame of each group that has enough stars is used. A reference you choose is stacked only with the group it belongs to; any other group is aligned to it and nothing more, which is how to line up an LRGB or narrowband set on one frame. If its size does not match a group, that group falls back to its own first frame.

Frames that cannot be used

A frame that cannot be opened, has a different size, or cannot be matched to the reference is skipped, and the log says why. If nothing aligns at all you get an error, never a stack of the reference alone.

Speed

Frames are read, aligned and added in parallel, and only running totals are kept, so memory use does not grow with the number of frames. The number of worker processes is limited by free memory.

With an NVIDIA GPU that SASpro’s hardware acceleration can reach, the debayer, star detection, warping and averaging run on the card, and only reading files and matching stars stay on the CPU. The window starts the GPU as soon as it opens (it takes several seconds), and shows which engine it will use beside the buttons. If the card cannot be used, or fails partway, the stack is done on the CPU instead and the log says why. Use hardware acceleration if available in Stacking Settings turns the GPU off.

What it leaves out

No bias or flat-dark frames of its own (a master flat should already be calibrated), no cosmetic correction, no rejection by quality, no weighting, no local normalization, no drizzle, no distortion correction. Use the Stacking Suite when you want those.
