Statistical Stretch

Take linear data to a non-linear display stretch by placing the background median exactly where you ask — analytically, in closed form. No iterative midtone search, no guess-and-check: the same inputs always produce the same result, bit for bit.

1. Using the tool

Adjust the controls, use Preview (or enable Auto-refresh preview) to see the result, then Apply. The preview supports zoom/pan; Clip stats reports how many pixels sit at or below the black point.

2. Technical details

Per channel the data is black-point subtracted, rescaled to [0,1], then passed through one closed-form transfer curve whose single parameter is solved analytically so the median lands on your target.

2.1 The midtones-transfer family

The stretch belongs to a one-parameter midtones-transfer family. For a pixel x ∈ [0,1] and a real-valued amount a:

f_a(x)=\frac{3^{a}\,x}{(3^{a}-1)\,x+1}

Here a analytically continues the effect of applying a midtones operator a times (the base 3 sets the per-unit strength). It is pinned at f_a(0)=0 and f_a(1)=1, and a=0 is the identity. This is not an arcsinh stretch — it is an analytic midtones transfer aimed precisely at a target median.

2.2 The amount that lands the median — solved, not searched

Given the measured (rescaled) median M and your requested target T, the amount that maps M → T is closed-form:

a=\frac{\ln\left(\dfrac{T}{1-T}\cdot\dfrac{1-M}{M}\right)}{\ln 3}

Because both M (measured) and T (chosen) are known before a single pixel is transformed, a is computed once in closed form — deterministic, perfectly reproducible, with no iterative midtone search and no recursive convergence. The median lands where you asked, the first time.

2.3 Black point (robust)

The background noise is estimated robustly from the lower half of the distribution (values at or below the median), so stars and bright structure can't inflate it:

\mathrm{noise}=1.4826\cdot\mathrm{MAD}\left(x\leq\mathrm{median}(x)\right)

The black point is then placed a chosen number of sigmas below the median (or, with No black clipping, at the image minimum):

b=\mathrm{clip}\left(\mathrm{median}(x)-\sigma\cdot\mathrm{noise},\;\min(x),\;0.99\right)

2.4 Rescale and exact median placement

Pixels are shifted/rescaled so the black point maps to 0 and 1 stays at 1, and the median is carried into the same space:

r=\frac{x-b}{1-b}\qquad M=\frac{\mathrm{median}-b}{1-b}

The evaluated kernel (the same family as §2.1, with the solved amount folded in) is:

S(r)=\frac{(M-1)\,T\,r}{M\,(T+r-1)-T\,r}

It is pinned through three points — and the middle one is the whole point of the tool:

S(0) = 0 (black stays black) S(1) = 1 (white stays white) S(M) = T (the measured median lands on your target, exactly)

Why S(M)=T holds exactly, by construction:

at r = M: numerator = (M - 1)·T·M denominator = M·(T + M - 1) - T·M = M² - M = M·(M - 1) S(M) = (M - 1)·T·M / [ M·(M - 1) ] = T

2.5 Colour handling

  • Linked — one black point and one median across all of RGB, the same curve applied to every channel, so colour balance is preserved.
  • Unlinked — black point, median and curve computed per channel, which neutralises a flat cast but can shift balance.
  • Luminance-only — a luminance L (profile weights, default Rec.709) is stretched with the same curve, then re-applied to the original linear RGB by per-pixel ratio, preserving chroma:
    \mathrm{out}=\mathrm{rgb}\cdot\frac{L_{\mathrm{stretched}}}{L}
    Luma blend β linearly mixes a normal linked stretch (β=0) with the luma-applied result (β=1).

2.6 Optional post-steps

Applied, in order, after the core stretch:

  • Curves boost — a 6-knot piecewise-linear S-curve pinned at black, median and white; the quarter-tone control points (strength c, target t) are
    p_{3x}=0.25\,(1-t)+t,\quad p_{3y}=p_{3x}^{\,1-c}
    p_{4x}=0.75\,(1-t)+t,\quad p_{4y}=\left(p_{4x}^{\,1-c}\right)^{\,1-c}
  • HDR highlight compress — a C1-continuous cubic-Hermite soft-knee above knee k; strength a sets the end-slope
    m_1=\mathrm{clip}(1+4a,\,1,\,5)
    (>1 compresses). For colour it is applied to luminance and recombined by linear scaling, which avoids star bloat.
  • Normalize — divide by the maximum so the brightest pixel is 1.0.

2.7 Notes

  • Runs per channel on float data in [0,1] (integer input is scaled by 255/65535; float with max > 5 is divided by its max first).
  • If the document has an active mask, the result is blended back against the source over the mask.
  • Clip stats reports pixels at or below the black point for the current mode — mono, linked/luma (on luminance), or unlinked (any channel).
